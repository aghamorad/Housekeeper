# Housekeeper — product truth

Who: Morad, on a 16-inch MacBook Pro in Tehran, behind a VPN, switching between
Persian and English. He calls himself "too much of a noob" and wants one app that
does the maintenance jobs without making him decode jargon.

What it is: a native macOS kitchen-sink utility — disk cleaner, uninstaller,
space lens, app updater, VPN tunnel manager, route tester, DNS switcher, local
model manager, background-activity reader.

Non-negotiables:
- Simple by default: Direct Clean sends cleaned items and uninstalled apps straight to macOS Trash; Quarantine Mode (`~/Housekeeping Quarantine`) is an opt-in safety net chosen once on first launch or toggled in Settings.
- Personal files, Apple containers, and AI model libraries remain strictly protected in both modes.
- The app never changes another app: no re-signing, no Gatekeeper changes.
- "Made by Morad" in the interface; every app has its own update check.
- Zero idle CPU when parked in the menu bar; lean RAM.
- A 75-year-old should be able to finish each job without help.

Real constraints: Iranian network, downloads can stall, app bundles arrive
signed, ad-hoc, or unsigned. Full Disk Access is granted by hand.
